# Fast Food Marketing Campaign Analysis
<br>

![Fast Food Image](/Image/Fast%20Food%20Image.jpg)


## Problem Background
The fast food industry has become one of the most competitive sectors over recent decades. Changing consumer habits, increasing demand for quick-service meals, and fierce market competition push fast food brands to innovate continuously to capture consumer interest.</br></br>
In the face of this strong competition, promotional campaigns have become a key strategy for attracting and retaining customer loyalty. However, selecting the most effective promotional approach remains a complex challenge.

## Business Understanding
Fast food marketing is critical for fast food brands and quick service restaurants to promote their products and services, attract brand loyalists, drive repeat purchases, and increase customer engagement and loyalty.</br></br>
According to survey of Food & Beverage marketers by Moengage, increasing customer engagement or loyalty is the top priority for the industry, with 46.8% of respondents listing it as most important to them.</br></br>
Ultimately, fast food marketing is a customer acquisition and retention strategy that is focused on driving engagement.

## Problem Statement
A Fast food company seeks to optimize sales and marketing strategies by analyzing campaign performance, market impact, and store-level performance. The goal is to identify the most effective promotions, evaluate market segments with the highest sales, and assess store performance based on sales, promotions, and store age groups. Insights from this analysis will drive data-driven strategies to maximize revenue and operational efficiency.

## Objective
1. Analyze Campaign Performance 
    - Identify the promotion that generates the highest sales 
    - Compare weekly sales performance over the span of four weeks 
    - Determine the most effective promotion type
<br>
2. Evaluate Market Impact
    - Identify the market size that generates the highest sales 
    - Compare sales performance based on promotions and market size
    - Develop optimal strategies tailored to each market segment
<br>
3. Assess Store-Level Performance 
    - Identify the store that generates the highest sales
    - Compare sales performance based on promotion and store age group
    - Compare sales performance based on promotion and each store age group
    - Develop optimal strategies tailored to each store age
<br>

## Analysis Result
Based on data analysis, the following are the main conclusions:

1. <b>Effective Promotion</b>
    - Promotion 3 has the best performance in increasing total sales, especially in stores aged 5-7 and 8-12 years.
    - Promotion 1 provides consistent results, especially in stores 1-4 years old, with performance not much different from Promotion 3.
    - Promotion 2 has the lowest performance compared to other promotions.

2. <b>Market Size</b>
    - Small Markets have the lowest total sales, with potential for growth through increased brand awareness and value-based promotions.
    - Mid-Market has high transaction volume, but lower average transaction value, indicating a large customer base but small purchases.
    - The Large market has the highest average transaction value, although transaction volume is lower than the Medium market.

3. <b>Store Age</b>
    - Stores 1-4 years old perform best in total and average sales, especially in Large markets.
    - Stores aged 5-7 years and 8-12 years show lower sales performance, with Promotion 3 being the best promotional strategy.
    - Stores 13-28 years old have average sales close to new stores (1-4 years), but total sales are lower, especially in Medium and Large markets.

<b>Strategy Recommendations</b>
1. <b>Strategy Based on Promotion</b>
    - Optimize Promotion 3:
        - Focus on 5-7 and 8-12 year old stores to drive sales in this age segment.
        - Expand the scope of this promotion to the Medium market to take advantage of potential transaction volume.
    
    - Strengthen Promotion 1:
        - Increase these promotions in new stores (1-4 years), especially in Large and Medium markets, to maintain initial sales momentum.

    - Evaluation and Revitalization Promotion 2:
        - Further analysis is needed to understand why this promotion was ineffective.
        - Consider strategy modifications, such as combining promotions with discounts or loyalty programs.

2. <b>Strategy Based on Market Size</b>
    - Small Market:
        - Increase brand awareness through targeted advertising and value-based promotions, such as special new customer discounts.
        - Introduce specific products that match these market preferences.

    - Medium Market:
        - Encourage large purchases with bundling promotions and discounts on larger transactions.
        - Implement loyalty programs to increase customer retention.
    
    - Big Market:
        - Launch targeted premium promotions, such as exclusive discounts or early access to new products.
        - Develop high-value products to drive greater spend per transaction.

3. <b>Strategy Based on Store Age</b>
    - Store Age 1-4 Years:
        - Accelerate new store expansion in Large markets to capitalize on high initial momentum.
        - Train the sales team to remain consistent in maintaining sales performance.
        
    - 5-7 Year Old and 8-12 Year Old Shop:
        - Increase customer engagement through loyalty programs and personal promotions.
        - Optimize needs-based promotional strategies, such as using Promotion 3 to increase total sales.
    
    - Shop Age 13-28 Years:
        - Reactivate existing customers through exclusive discounts and loyalty campaigns.
        - Rebrand to update the appeal of old stores, especially in Medium and Large markets.

By implementing this strategy, the company can maximize sales potential in each market segment and store age, while increasing the effectiveness of the promotions used.
